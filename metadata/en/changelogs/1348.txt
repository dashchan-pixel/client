* Posting: the submission form now floats over the thread it replies to instead of replacing it. Drag the sheet down to half height or swipe it away to read the posts you are answering — the thread behind is dimmed by exactly as much of it as the form covers — and the captcha and the send button stay pinned at the bottom at whatever height the sheet has settled at. The section headers are gone, the comment field takes the height a taller sheet gives it, and the attach action sits at the end of the row of checkboxes. The full-screen form is still there as User interface → "Classic style". A post card opened from a reflink is a window of its own and would stand over the form, so the cards step aside while it is up and come back afterwards exactly as they stood; coming back from a screen the form pushed — the commands editor, a forum's proxy settings — lands on the sheet again rather than on the page under it.
* Posting: the form reuses the captcha you just solved. Opening the submission form used to solve a reCAPTCHA or hCaptcha again every single time, while the drafts storage sat on the one that had just been solved — leave the form and come back a second later, solve it again. A forum that answers "there is a captcha here, press to load it" — 2ch's emoji captcha — is restored the same way, so opening the form no longer spends two round trips reading a button. The reuse window is two minutes rather than five: that is how long a reCAPTCHA or hCaptcha token is actually valid, and handed back at four minutes it was dead on arrival.
* The two things a page is on screen to be used for move down to the thumb. A small bar floating in the bottom corner carries a thread's Reply and Contents and a board's New thread and Refresh, painted in the toolbar's colour and carrying its icons; where you have written any, a ⌘ joins them for the App commands the forum and board are in scope of. A long press and a drag reorders the buttons, a two-finger tap turns the bar between horizontal and vertical, and an item the page hides is no more in the bar than in the toolbar.
* Captcha: reCAPTCHA 3. Version 3 shows no challenge — it scores the client behind the page — so the token is minted in the background and reported as a solved captcha. The challenge is run inside the site's own page, with its cookies and its history, which is where the site runs it and what it is scored against: a page assembled to be judged, carrying nothing but the script being asked for, scores at the floor and has its token refused. Extensions pass the action the token is minted under per request.
* Every forum keeps a ban log: each post refused as banned is stored with its board, reason, expiration and the address the forum saw at the time, under "Ban log" in that forum's Additional section — details on tap, copy or delete on long press, expired entries dimmed, clear from the toolbar. Before a fresh captcha is requested the log is asked whether the address the forum sees right now is already banned on the board being posted to, and a toast says so rather than a captcha being spent minting a token for a post that will only be refused again.
* Visible IP: the row that reports the address a forum sees can now change it. Flag a command "Run to change visible IP" for that forum and the row names it, with a button that runs it — from the forum's settings, from the toast a banned post puts up, or from a script — and the pooled connections are dropped afterwards, so nothing carries on talking over a connection opened at the old address. No proxy service has any code in the app: ready-made commands for Asocks and DataImpulse ship in docs/commands, importable, with the credentials kept in the command environment so a command can be exported without them. Long press the row to copy the address a ban appeal asks for, long press the command row to open it for editing.
* A forum's proxy can be limited to what you send: with "Proxy for sending only", posts and reports — together with their firewall resolution and their captcha — go through the proxy, while browsing, thumbnails and files load directly. The captcha travels with the post rather than around it because one solved on the direct address and submitted from the proxy is usually rejected. A proxy takes a login and a password now, a service that authorises by credentials having no practical way to whitelist a phone's address.
* Commands reach the app, the forum and a store of their own. `app` is the application's settings, by the key the settings screens store them under; `chan` is the forum the run belongs to and that forum's cookies — the same store "Manage cookies" edits, so a clearance a script obtains is one the whole app then uses; `store` is the command's own key→value store, which is what a script that has to remember something between runs was otherwise driven to squat in a setting for. The IP a forum sees and the proxy its traffic takes sit on `chan` beside the cookies but are granted apart, being the one thing here that reaches off the device. Each is granted per command in that command's editor, and an imported command arrives with the default grants whatever its document asks for. A setting only read while a screen is being built — the themes, the scales, the language, the expanded screen — is now applied at the end of the run instead of sitting in the file unseen.
* Commands can put a question to the user: show, confirm, prompt, choose and chooseMany raise a dialog mid-run and hand back what was chosen, or null for one that was cancelled. The run stands still until it is answered, with its deadline widened to five minutes while a dialog is up. alert, confirm and prompt are bound to the same dialogs — a WebView with nothing handling them cancels every one, so confirm() had been answering "no" without ever asking.
* Commands gain an App target, for a script that operates on nothing and does its work through what it was granted; it is runnable from the Commands screen, and it is what the app runs when it needs another visible IP. Behind the proxy grant, a script can read the IP and location a forum sees, read and write that forum's proxy as one connection string — scheme://user:password@host:port, the form every service hands its endpoint out on — and ask for the visible-IP command to be run, waiting on the answer since it asks for another IP in order to use it.
* Toasts stack instead of covering one another. The oldest keeps the usual position and every later one stacks above it, up to three, so a warning landing a moment after a failure no longer takes its Details button away before it can be tapped.
* 4chan hands out captchas again. A header's case is the protocol's choice rather than the server's — HTTP/2 requires lowercase — so every lookup written in the familiar spelling (CF-RAY, Server, Content-Type, Content-Range) silently found nothing on exactly the hosts modern enough to negotiate HTTP/2, leaving a Cloudflare challenge unrecognised and so unsolved. Cloudflare's "Verification Required" title is now recognised too.
* A forum's Connection settings take a User-Agent of their own; left empty, the WebView default is kept.
* References to deleted posts are links now: a bare >>NNN that the forum emitted as plain greentext is linkified and marked X when it resolves to a post known to be deleted, mirroring the Y that marks your own, and tapping it opens the thread's last-seen copy instead of a single-post fetch that would 404.
* Threadshots no longer crash on a post carrying a country flag or an extension's decorator icon.
* Extensions: a drag-slider captcha — the user drags a puzzle piece across a background image — instead of faking one through image multiple-choice, and a TAG_SECRET markup tag drawn in the theme's capcode colour with a posting-form button that wraps the selection in it.
* Ripples take the corner radius of the surface under them, on the floating bar's buttons and on the app's own buttons, and the ⌘ menu gets its shadow, rows themed to read against their own card, and a width that hugs what is in it.
